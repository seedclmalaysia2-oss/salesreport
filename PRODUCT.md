# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Six people sign in: five salespeople (Alan, Dino, Khen, Sakinah, Simon) and one admin account, Seed Malaysia, which also carries its own sales figures. Everyone sees every team's numbers by default; the admin can restrict an individual to their own rows when there's a reason to.

They are **phone-first**. A rep checks this between customer visits, on the road, in whatever light they happen to be in — not sitting at a desk with a spreadsheet open. Desktop matters for the admin's monthly workbook upload, but the reading experience belongs to the phone.

The admin has a second job the reps don't: uploading the source .xlsx workbooks, keeping the dataset current, and controlling who can see what.

## Product Purpose

Turns raw sales workbooks into a live picture of how the team is performing, covering customer-level and brand-level sales from 2022 onward, against monthly targets per rep and for the team.

The job it is actually hired for is narrower than "show the numbers": people open it to find out **what changed or went wrong**. A customer who has stopped ordering, a brand that is slipping, a rep drifting behind target. Totals are context for that question, not the answer to it.

Success is a problem being caught while there is still time to act on it, rather than surfacing at month-end when the quarter is already decided.

## Positioning

It catches problems early. Anything else the dashboard shows is in service of noticing a decline before it becomes a loss.

## Brand Personality

Sharp and fast. A professional instrument for people who already know their business — it should not explain sales to a salesperson. Dense where density earns its place, quick to read, and confident enough to state a bad number without softening it.

The tone is factual rather than encouraging. No congratulation, no cheerleading, no framing a shortfall as an opportunity. The reader draws their own conclusion from an honest number.

## Anti-references

Not a **spreadsheet dump**: walls of raw figures with no hierarchy, where every number carries the same visual weight and the reader has to do the analysis themselves. If everything is equally prominent, nothing has been designed.

Not **heavy corporate BI** (Power BI, Tableau): filter panels flanking every view, chrome competing with content, slow to load, and requiring training before a rep can answer a simple question. No one here is going on a course to read their own sales figures.

## Design Principles

**Surface the exception, not the total.** The default view should answer "what needs my attention?" before it answers "what is the total?". A number that has not moved is rarely the story.

**The phone is the primary instrument.** Every decision gets judged at 375px first and on a desktop second. A view that only resolves on a wide monitor has failed the person it was built for.

**No training required.** A rep should get their answer on first use without a walkthrough. Familiar patterns beat clever ones; the interface should disappear into the question being asked.

**Hierarchy is the product.** The value added over the source workbook is deciding what matters most. Rank, group, and de-emphasise deliberately — refusing to choose just hands the spreadsheet back to the reader.

**State it plainly.** Report bad numbers as directly as good ones. Precision and restraint build the trust that makes the tool worth opening.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA is the bar: body text at 4.5:1 contrast, large text at 3:1, full keyboard access, and visible focus states. AA also covers use of colour (SC 1.4.1) — colour alone must never be the only way information is conveyed.

**Readability in sunlight is a stated requirement**, following from the phone-first context. Reps read this outdoors and in cars, so contrast needs headroom above the minimum and a high-contrast light theme must be genuinely usable, not a token alternative to the dark default.
